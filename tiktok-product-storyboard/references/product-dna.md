# Product DNA from Images

Use all supplied product images together. Distinguish visible evidence from inference and leave hidden details unspecified.

## Extraction record

1. **Category and function** — neutral object class and only demonstrated or user-provided use.
2. **Silhouette** — overall outline, dominant shape, corners/curves, taper, profile symmetry, and stable negative spaces.
3. **Proportions** — height-to-width relationship, part-to-body scale, thickness, opening size, and approximate hand-relative scale. Avoid invented measurements.
4. **Colors and finish** — base color, accent color, transparency, gloss/matte finish, and which surface owns each color.
5. **Materials** — visible material cues such as brushed metal, clear plastic, silicone, fabric, or glass; mark uncertain material as visual appearance, not fact.
6. **Parts** — lid, cap, hinge, latch, handle, spout, port, cable, button, display, liner, tray, blade, seal, feet, and removable pieces actually visible.
7. **Working surfaces** — surfaces that pour, cut, heat, hold, seal, press, connect, open, or receive another object.
8. **Grip zones** — physically plausible hand placement, grip direction, finger clearance, and areas that must remain visible during operation.
9. **Do-not-occlude zones** — proof action, opening, control, interface, logo/label needed for identity, moving joint, or contact point.
10. **Executable actions** — only actions supported by geometry, supplied copy, or reference evidence.
11. **Prohibited errors** — likely failure modes specific to this object: extra handle, missing liner, wrong hinge direction, mirrored port, incorrect lid, warped opening, floating accessory, or changing scale/color.

## Compact DNA block

Before prompting, write a reusable block:

`Product identity | silhouette/proportion | colors/material appearance | required parts | working surface | allowed grip/contact | allowed actions | must-remain-visible zones | forbidden mutations | unsupported/unknown facts`

This block governs every prompt and storyboard. Product packaging text may remain as photographed but must not be rewritten, regenerated as new copy, or treated as a verified claim.
